# AICO-Intellig Theory Innovation Blueprint

## 1. Goal

The paper should not be positioned as a generic algorithm-selection study with extra engineering experiments. If we want a stronger theoretical contribution, the central object must be a new decision problem:

> **budgeted relative-gain routing with abstention under expensive black-box optimization**

The point is not merely to predict the best optimizer. The point is to decide, after observing a short endogenous diagnostic trajectory from a stable base optimizer, whether switching away from that base optimizer is justified under asymmetric downside risk.

This document defines the theory track that is most consistent with the current method and current evidence.

## 2. Core Formal Object

Let:

- `x` denote a problem instance drawn from an instance space `\mathcal X`
- `A_0` denote the default base optimizer
- `\mathcal A = \{A_1,\dots,A_M\}` denote candidate optimizers
- `T_K(x)` denote the endogenous trajectory generated by running `A_0` for the first `K` evaluations
- `\phi_K(x)` denote the trajectory-derived feature map computed from `T_K(x)`
- `G(a, x; B)` denote the final optimization utility of algorithm `a` on instance `x` under total budget `B`

Define the **relative net gain** of switching from `A_0` to candidate `a` after `K` diagnostic evaluations:

\[
\Delta_K(a, x)
=
G\bigl(a, x; B-K\bigr) - G\bigl(A_0, x; B\bigr) - C_{\mathrm{sw}}(a, K, x),
\]

where `C_sw` is the switching cost, which may include:

- orchestration cost,
- state-transfer cost,
- compatibility penalty,
- or an abstract switching surcharge.

The routing policy is a map

\[
\pi_K:\phi_K(x)\mapsto \{A_0\}\cup \mathcal A,
\]

where choosing `A_0` means **abstain / stay with the default**.

This already distinguishes the problem from standard top-1 algorithm selection:

- standard algorithm selection solves a best-label prediction problem;
- our problem solves a **relative-gain decision with abstention**.

## 3. Theory Axis I: Decision-Theoretic Reframing

### 3.1 Bayes-Optimal Routing Rule

Given diagnostic evidence `Z_K = \phi_K(x)`, define

\[
\mu_a(Z_K) = \mathbb E[\Delta_K(a, x)\mid Z_K].
\]

The expected utility of a routing policy is

\[
\mathcal U(\pi_K)=
\mathbb E\bigl[\Delta_K(\pi_K(Z_K), x)\bigr],
\]

with the convention that `\Delta_K(A_0, x)=0`.

### Proposition 1 (Bayes-Optimal Relative-Gain Router)

For the above utility, the Bayes-optimal policy is

\[
\pi_K^\star(Z_K)
=
\begin{cases}
\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\mu_a(Z_K), & \text{if } \max_{a\in\mathcal A}\mu_a(Z_K) > 0,\\
A_0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]

#### Why this matters

This proposition is simple but foundational. It proves that once the deployment target is relative net gain with abstention, the optimal decision rule is **not** top-1 classification. The optimal rule is a **positive-threshold decision on conditional expected gain**.

#### Proof sketch

Condition on `Z_K`. Since choosing `A_0` yields utility `0`, any candidate optimizer with negative conditional expected gain is dominated by abstention. Hence the conditional maximizer is either the best positive-gain candidate or the default optimizer.

#### Theoretical value

This is the correct first theorem because it formalizes the paper's main conceptual shift:

- from absolute label prediction,
- to asymmetric utility maximization with abstention.

### Corollary 1 (Top-1 Misalignment)

A classifier that minimizes 0-1 top-1 error is generally not Bayes-optimal for maximizing `\mathcal U(\pi_K)` unless the ranking of class probabilities coincides with the ranking of conditional relative gains and all incorrect switches have symmetric cost.

#### Interpretation

This gives a precise mathematical reason why static top-1 selection can look reasonable under conventional accuracy metrics yet still yield negative expected optimization gain.

## 4. Theory Axis II: One-Sided Conformal Abstention

The paper should not claim more than standard conformal theory can support. The strongest clean statement is not "the harmful switch probability is always below `\alpha` conditional on switching." That statement is usually too strong. The correct direction is a **marginal one-sided error control** statement.

Let:

- `\widehat \Delta_a(Z_K)` be the predicted net gain for candidate `a`
- `R = \widehat \Delta_a(Z_K)-\Delta_K(a,x)` be the over-prediction residual
- `q_{1-\alpha}` be the one-sided empirical conformal quantile estimated on a calibration set

Define the lower bound

\[
L_a(Z_K)=\widehat \Delta_a(Z_K)-q_{1-\alpha}.
\]

The conformal gate switches only if

\[
\max_{a\in\mathcal A} L_a(Z_K) > 0.
\]

### Proposition 2 (Marginal Harmful-Switch Control)

Assume split conformal calibration is valid for the residual of the selected candidate score. Then

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\Delta_K(\pi_K(Z_K),x) < 0 \ \wedge\  \pi_K(Z_K)\neq A_0 \bigr)\le \alpha.
\]

#### Why this statement is correct

If the policy switches, then it has certified a positive lower bound. A harmful switch means the realized gain is negative. That event implies the lower bound was violated, which is exactly the one-sided conformal error event.

#### Important nuance

This is a **marginal** guarantee, not a conditional one. In particular, it does **not** directly imply

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\Delta_K<0\mid \pi_K\neq A_0\bigr)\le \alpha.
\]

What we can infer is

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\Delta_K<0\mid \pi_K\neq A_0\bigr)
\le
\frac{\alpha}{\mathbb P(\pi_K\neq A_0)},
\]

whenever the denominator is nonzero.

This distinction is crucial. It keeps the theory correct and prevents overclaiming.

### Proposition 3 (Selective Risk-Return Frontier)

Under the same one-sided conformal construction, as `\alpha` decreases, the switch set becomes nested:

\[
\mathcal S_{\alpha_1}\subseteq \mathcal S_{\alpha_2}, \qquad \alpha_1 < \alpha_2,
\]

where

\[
\mathcal S_\alpha = \{x : \max_a L_a(\phi_K(x)) > 0\}.
\]

Consequently:

- switch rate is monotone nondecreasing in `\alpha`,
- abstention rate is monotone nonincreasing in `\alpha`.

#### Why this matters

This proposition turns the empirical `alpha` sweep into a genuine theory-backed claim: the conformal gate induces a nested family of selective routing policies, hence a controllable risk-return frontier.

#### Scope

This is primarily a structural theorem. It does not claim monotonicity of mean gain, because mean gain depends on the underlying data distribution.

## 5. Theory Axis III: Budget-Neutral Diagnostic Optimality

The `K`-step diagnostic window is not just a hyperparameter. It should be elevated into a theoretical object.

Define the routing value after `K` diagnostic evaluations:

\[
V(K)=\sup_{\pi_K}\mathbb E[\Delta_K(\pi_K(\phi_K(x)),x)].
\]

This value depends on two competing forces:

1. **information gain**: larger `K` improves diagnostic evidence and reduces routing uncertainty;
2. **budget squeeze**: larger `K` leaves less remaining budget for the switched optimizer.

### Assumption A (Information/Budget Trade-Off)

Suppose:

- the information value term `I(K)` is increasing and concave,
- the opportunity cost term `C(K)` is increasing and convex,
- and `V(K)=I(K)-C(K)`.

### Proposition 4 (Existence of an Interior Optimal Diagnostic Window)

Under Assumption A, if

\[
I'(0)>C'(0)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lim_{K\to K_{\max}} I'(K) < \lim_{K\to K_{\max}} C'(K),
\]

then there exists at least one interior optimizer

\[
K^\star \in (0,K_{\max})
\]

such that `V(K^\star)` is maximal.

#### Why this matters

This proposition gives the `K` ablation a theoretical interpretation:

- too small `K`: insufficient evidence;
- too large `K`: excessive diagnostic budget consumption;
- intermediate `K`: optimal balance.

#### Value

This is not a generic tuning statement. It formalizes the central claim that endogenous diagnosis is useful only because it trades information against remaining optimization budget in a principled way.

## 6. Theory Axis IV: Scale-Invariant Endogenous Features

The paper already emphasizes scale-normalized trajectory features. This can support a modest but clean structural proposition.

Let the objective be transformed affinely:

\[
\widetilde f(x)=a f(x)+b,\qquad a>0.
\]

If the trajectory features are constructed from ratios, normalized improvements, or rank-preserving quantities, then many of them remain invariant to positive affine transformation.

### Proposition 5 (Positive Affine Invariance of Normalized Trajectory Features)

Suppose each component of `\phi_K` is built from:

- normalized objective differences,
- normalized step-length ratios,
- rank-based progress statistics,
- or other positive affine invariant quantities.

Then for any `a>0` and any offset `b`,

\[
\phi_K(\widetilde f)=\phi_K(f).
\]

#### Why this matters

This proposition supports the claim that trajectory diagnostics transfer better across heterogeneous physical families with different objective scales and units.

#### Status

This is a weaker theorem than the previous three, but it is easy to make rigorous and aligns well with the current feature design.

## 7. What Can Be Claimed as Theoretical Innovation

The most defensible theory package is the following:

### Main Theory Claim

Algorithm routing in expensive PDE optimization should be formulated as a **budgeted relative-gain decision with abstention**, not as absolute top-1 prediction.

### Supporting Theory Claims

1. The Bayes-optimal router is a positive-threshold rule on conditional relative gain.
2. One-sided conformal lower-bound gating yields marginal harmful-switch control and a nested abstention frontier.
3. The diagnostic window `K` admits an information-versus-budget trade-off with an interior optimum under mild structural assumptions.
4. Scale-normalized endogenous trajectory features are invariant to positive affine objective transformations.

This bundle is substantially stronger than saying "we use conformal prediction" or "we predict gains with CatBoost."

## 8. What Should Not Be Claimed

To stay mathematically credible, the paper should **not** claim:

- conditional harmful-switch probability is always bounded by `\alpha`,
- strong conformal validity under arbitrary LOFO covariate shift without additional assumptions,
- universal superiority of trajectory features over static features,
- or universal monotonic improvement of mean gain as `\alpha` decreases or `K` increases.

These would overreach beyond what current theory and current experiments support.

## 9. New Experiments Needed for the Theory Track

If the paper is to emphasize theory, the experiments should be reorganized to validate the propositions rather than merely compare baselines.

### E1. Utility Misalignment Experiment

Goal:

- Show that top-1 accuracy and deployment utility diverge.

Design:

- Report accuracy, mean gain, and loss rate together for the same routing models.
- Construct at least one case where a higher-accuracy classifier has lower deployment utility.

Supports:

- Proposition 1 and Corollary 1.

### E2. Marginal Harmful-Switch Calibration Check

Goal:

- Empirically test whether the observed harmful-switch frequency tracks the one-sided conformal target.

Design:

- For `\alpha \in \{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3\}`, report:
  - switch rate,
  - harmful-switch mass,
  - harmful-switch rate conditional on switching,
  - mean gain,
  - CVaR.

Supports:

- Proposition 2 and Proposition 3.

### E3. K Trade-Off Decomposition

Goal:

- Show the gain-risk-budget trade-off behind the interior-optimal `K`.

Design:

- In addition to mean gain and loss rate, report:
  - remaining budget fraction after diagnosis,
  - switch rate,
  - conditional gain given switching.

Supports:

- Proposition 4.

### E4. Scale Robustness Check

Goal:

- Verify that normalized trajectory features remain stable under affine rescaling.

Design:

- For a subset of instances, rescale the objective by `a` and `b`,
- recompute features,
- report relative deviation.

Supports:

- Proposition 5.

## 10. Suggested Theory Section Structure

If we decide to push the paper in a stronger theoretical direction, the theory section should be organized as:

1. **Problem formulation**
   - budgeted relative-gain routing with abstention

2. **Optimal decision rule**
   - Bayes-optimal positive-threshold router
   - top-1 misalignment corollary

3. **Risk-controlled switching**
   - one-sided conformal lower-bound gate
   - marginal harmful-switch control
   - nested `alpha` frontier

4. **Budgeted diagnosis**
   - value of information versus budget squeeze
   - existence of an interior `K^\star`

5. **Invariant endogenous representation**
   - positive affine invariance proposition

This is a much more coherent theory story than attaching a generic regret bound or a generic conformal lemma.

## 11. Recommendation

The best path is **not** to force the paper into a purely theoretical style. The better target is:

> a method paper with a genuine theoretical core

That means:

- the paper still depends on strong engineering LOFO experiments,
- but the contribution is elevated by a clean decision-theoretic formulation and two to four nontrivial propositions that are actually about this problem.

Among all candidate theory directions, the most important one is:

> **relative-gain routing with abstention is the correct Bayes decision problem for expensive algorithm switching**

This is the most original and most defensible theoretical center of the work.
